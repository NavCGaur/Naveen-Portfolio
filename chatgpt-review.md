Given what showed up in your data — the duplicate-event bug, unclassified bot/crawler traffic, and vanity metrics like raw bounce rate — here's a prioritized punch list to bring the dashboard up to modern standard.

Tier 1: Fix data integrity first (everything downstream depends on this)
Dedupe the pageview firing bug on /blog — debounce or gate the event so route re-renders don't log multiple hits per real page load.
Server-side session stitching — assign a session ID at first touch and dedupe by (session_id, path) within a short window instead of counting every event tag as a new pageview.
Bot/crawler classification layer — you're already building this for AEO, so extend it here: tag traffic by ASN/ISP (Google, Facebook, Amazon, Tencent Cloud, Datacamp, M247, Hetzner, DigitalOcean, Huawei Cloud) and known crawler UAs, and store it as a traffic_type column (human, search_bot, ai_bot, datacenter_proxy, unknown) rather than silently mixing it into "visits."
Separate infra noise from content metrics — /robots.txt, /.well-known/\*, /xmlrpc.php, /.git/config should never hit your main pageview/bounce calculations. Route them to a "security/infra events" table instead.
IP reputation / scanner filtering — auto-flag sessions hitting /.git/config, /xmlrpc.php, /wp-admin, etc. as scan attempts; exclude from analytics, optionally feed into a blocklist for your VM firewall.
Tier 2: Modernize the core metrics
Replace bounce rate with engagement rate as the primary quality signal (this is what GA4 moved to) — define "engaged" as session ≥10s, ≥2 pageviews, or a conversion event. Bounce rate alone is easy to game/misread, as you saw with /blog.
Median session duration alongside average — a few 300s+ outlier sessions (Brazil, Ukraine) are skewing your averages upward.
Scroll depth + read completion for blog posts — you have rich duration data already; scroll depth tells you if people are actually reading vs. leaving the tab open.
Exit rate per page, not just bounce rate — shows where people leave after engaging, which is different from bouncing immediately.
New vs. returning visitor split — you're tracking IP-level identity already; this is a cheap addition with high signal value.
Tier 3: Acquisition & attribution
UTM parameter capture — right now everything non-organic/non-Twitter falls into "Direct," which hides real campaign performance. Add UTM parsing for anything you promote (LinkedIn, newsletter, Upwork proposals).
Channel grouping logic — bucket referrers into Organic Search / Paid / Social / Referral / Direct / AI Assistant properly, instead of raw source strings.
AI referral vs. AI crawler distinction — separate "ChatGPT/Perplexity/Claude sent a human here" (referrer-based) from "an AI crawler indexed this page" (UA-based). This directly supports your AEO thesis and is something most dashboards (even GA4) don't do well — a differentiator for your own tooling.
Tier 4: Content & conversion tracking
Funnel view for /free-audit — entry → form interaction → submission → report view. You already see 500s avg duration there; a funnel tells you where drop-off happens.
CTA click tracking — instrument key buttons (audit CTA, contact, testimonials) as events, not just pageviews.
Outbound link tracking — useful for case studies/portfolio links.

Tier 5: Operational / dashboard UX

Anomaly detection + annotations — flag traffic spikes automatically (like the June 30 datacenter-IP surge) and let you annotate the timeline with deploys or content publishes, so spikes/dips are explainable at a glance.
Period-over-period comparison built into every widget, not just the overview card (you already do this for overview — extend it to top pages, geo, referrers).
Retention/cohort view — do people who read one article come back? Valuable for judging whether content strategy is compounding.
Live visitor map/feed refinement — you have lastSeen: "live" already; pair it with the bot-classification layer so the live view isn't dominated by crawlers.
